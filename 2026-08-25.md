## New Research Publications

**2026-08-24** (PubMed entry date) — "Liquid biopsies reveal dual compartments of cancer risk from tumor and host-derived mutations."
*Bioinformatics (Oxford, England)*, 2026 Aug 1; 42(Supplement_2): btag491. DOI: [10.1093/bioinformatics/btag491](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btag491). PMID: 42635246.

**Summary:** This large-scale bioinformatics study analyzed over 9,000 patients with CHIP (clonal hematopoiesis of indeterminate potential) data and 1,500 patients with ctDNA data across solid tumors profiled at Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center. The authors found that CHIP mutations — somatic mutations arising in age-related hematopoietic clones, distinct from germline inherited variants — in DNA damage response genes including **CHEK2**, PPM1D, ATM, and TP53, are associated with worse overall survival, increased metastatic potential, and site-specific dissemination. Joint modeling in lung adenocarcinoma confirmed the independent prognostic contributions of both CHIP (host-derived) and ctDNA (tumor-derived) mutations.

**Variant class:** Not applicable — this study concerns **somatic** CHEK2 mutations in hematopoietic clones (CHIP), not germline pathogenic variants such as c.1100delC (truncating) or p.Ile157Thr / p.Ser428Phe (missense).

**Why it matters for CHEK2 carriers:** This study does **not** change the established baseline risk estimates for germline CHEK2 carriers (truncating variants: ~2.66× breast cancer OR, 20–30% lifetime breast risk; I157T/S428F low-penetrance OR ~1.3; ~2× prostate risk). The findings pertain to somatic CHIP-derived CHEK2 mutations detected in blood as prognostic biomarkers in cancer patients, which is a separate biological and clinical context from hereditary CHEK2-related cancer predisposition.
