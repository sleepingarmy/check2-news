# CHEK2 Daily Report — 2026-09-30

## TL;DR
A multicenter neuropathology study in *Brain Pathology* validating a newly proposed glioma subtype (GPAP) found that 5 of 20 patients carried germline pathogenic variants in tumor-predisposition genes — including one CHEK2 carrier. It's a single case in a small cohort, so it doesn't change any established risk figures, but it adds to a slowly growing literature asking whether CHEK2 plays a role in brain-tumor predisposition.

## New Research Publications

**2026-09-29 — "Gliomas with pleomorphic and pseudopapillary features (GPAP) are circumscribed tumors with targetable mutations, prolonged survival, and frequent tumor predisposition"** — *Brain Pathology* — [DOI: 10.1111/bpa.70153](https://doi.org/10.1111/bpa.70153)

A French-Italian consortium validated "GPAP" (gliomas with pleomorphic and pseudopapillary features) as a distinct, well-circumscribed glioma entity with 85% 10-year survival despite sometimes high-grade histology. Among 20 patients, 5 carried germline pathogenic variants in hereditary tumor genes: MLH1, BRCA2, **CHEK2**, NF2, and RB1 — suggesting this tumor type may warrant germline testing. For CHEK2 carriers, one glioma in a 20-case series is far too little to infer a brain-tumor risk association, and no baseline risk estimate changes; it's noted here as a signal to watch, since CHEK2 germline links to brain tumors remain unestablished.
