# CHEK2 Daily Report — 2026-08-27

## TL;DR

The most clinically relevant item today is a multi-institutional study in the *International Journal of Gynecological Cancer* of risk-reducing gynecologic surgery in carriers of non-BRCA predisposition genes, including CHEK2: among 207 low/moderate-risk carriers, 97% of prophylactic specimens were benign and no high-grade serous carcinoma or STIC was found — reinforcing that CHEK2 is not an established ovarian cancer gene and that risk-reducing salpingo-oophorectomy is generally not indicated for CHEK2 carriers on that basis. A second paper in *Cancers* (Basel) found CHEK2 among the most common somatic DNA-damage-response mutations in lung cancer (19.4% of DDR-mutant tumors), with no overall-survival effect for DDR status as a whole. Neither finding changes established germline CHEK2 risk estimates.

## New Research Publications

**2026-08-07** (online ahead of print) — "Risk-reducing gynecologic surgery in non-BRCA cancer predisposition mutations: a multi-institutional study."
*International Journal of Gynecological Cancer*, 2026:104954. DOI: [10.1016/j.ijgc.2026.104954](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijgc.2026.104954). PMID: 42648957.

**Summary:** This retrospective study across three academic centers (University of New Mexico, Baylor Scott & White, Brigham and Women's/Harvard) examined 207 patients with pathogenic germline variants in low- to moderate-risk genes — PALB2, BRIP1, RAD51D, RAD51C, ATM, CHEK2 (3% of the cohort), and BARD1 — who underwent risk-reducing salpingectomy or salpingo-oophorectomy between 2013 and 2022. Only three serous borderline tumors (1%) were found; 97% of specimens were benign, with zero serous tubal intra-epithelial carcinoma (STIC) or high-grade serous carcinoma, and all patients had no evidence of disease at median 17-month follow-up. By contrast, the comparison BRCA1/2 cohort (n=388) had pathologic findings in 5%, including high-grade serous carcinoma.

**Variant class:** Mixed/not specified by gene — CHEK2 carriers comprised ~3% of the cohort (variant-level detail not broken out in the abstract).

**Why it matters for CHEK2 carriers:** This is the largest reported cohort addressing prophylactic gynecologic surgery in moderate-penetrance genes and supports current guidance that CHEK2 pathogenic variants do **not** confer a meaningfully elevated ovarian cancer risk warranting risk-reducing surgery. It does not change the established breast (~2–2.7× OR for truncating variants, 20–30% lifetime risk) or prostate (~2×) risk estimates, nor the low-penetrance classification of p.Ile157Thr / p.Ser428Phe (OR ~1.3).

Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42648957/

**2026-08-13** — "The Association of Social and Biological Factors with Clinical Outcomes in Patients with Lung Cancer and DNA Damage Repair Pathway Mutations: A Single-Institution Experience."
*Cancers (Basel)*, 2026 Aug 13;18(16):2606. DOI: [10.3390/cancers18162606](https://doi.org/10.3390/cancers18162606). PMID: 42649919.

**Summary:** In 232 lung cancer patients (96% NSCLC) at University of Illinois Chicago, 29% had tumor DNA-damage-response (DDR) mutations, with **CHEK2 the second most common** DDR gene altered (19.4% of DDR-mutant tumors, after ARID1A). DDR-mutant tumors had higher tumor mutational burden and were enriched for adenocarcinoma histology, but DDR status overall was not associated with survival; social determinants (food insecurity, lack of insurance) were stronger outcome predictors.

**Variant class:** Not applicable — these are **somatic/tumor** alterations (germline vs. somatic not separated in the abstract), not germline c.1100delC or missense carrier findings.

**Why it matters for CHEK2 carriers:** No change to germline risk estimates. This is a tumor-profiling study; it does not address inherited CHEK2 predisposition to lung cancer, which remains unestablished.

Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42649919/

## Clinical Trials Updates

**NCT05420064** — "An Intervention to Increase Genetic Testing in Families Who May Share a Gene Mutation Related to Cancer Risk" (EfFORT trial) — record updated **2026-08-27**, status: Recruiting. This study evaluates interventions to increase **cascade genetic testing** — offering testing to relatives of people found to carry an inherited cancer-risk variant — and explicitly includes **CHEK2 gene mutation** families among its 23 eligible conditions, alongside BRCA1/2, PALB2, ATM, Lynch syndrome genes, and others. A second embedded trial addresses keeping patients and primary care providers updated about variants of uncertain significance. Relevant to CHEK2 families because cascade uptake remains a major gap in realizing the benefit of identifying carriers.
Link: https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05420064
