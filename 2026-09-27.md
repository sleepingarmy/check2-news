# CHEK2 Daily Report — 2026-09-27

## TL;DR
Two new papers today. A prospective study of 452 metastatic prostate cancer patients (PROMPT) shows that using tumour DNA testing to triage patients for germline testing detects far more carriers of pathogenic variants in BRCA1/2, PALB2, ATM, and CHEK2 than family-history-based referral — and at roughly one-twelfth the cost per carrier found. Separately, a small Pakistani early-onset breast cancer cohort found pathogenic truncating CHEK2 variants in 4.5% of patients, underscoring that CHEK2 contributes to hereditary breast cancer in under-studied non-European populations.

## New Research Publications

**2026-09-19 — "Tumour-first DNA testing as a gateway to germline screening in patients with metastatic prostate cancer" — *European Journal of Cancer*** ([PMID 42800309](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42800309/) | [DOI 10.1016/j.ejca.2026.117060](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejca.2026.117060))
In the prospective PROMPT study (452 metastatic prostate cancer patients), 7.8% carried germline pathogenic variants in BRCA2, BRCA1, PALB2, ATM, or CHEK2. A tumour-first workflow — using tumour sequencing to decide who gets germline testing — identified 22 carriers versus only 8 under a modelled family-history-based referral strategy, with higher precision (PPV 51% vs 13%) and far lower cost per detected carrier (€998 vs €12,246). For CHEK2 carriers and families, this supports broader tumour-informed germline screening in prostate cancer rather than relying on family history alone, which misses most carriers.

**2026-09-22 — "Spectrum of germline variants in moderate to high penetrance non-BRCA breast cancer susceptibility genes in a Pakistani early-onset cohort" — *Cancer Genetics*** ([PMID 42800083](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42800083/) | [DOI 10.1016/j.cancergen.2026.09.009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cancergen.2026.09.009))
Among 66 early-onset breast cancer patients in Pakistan, two pathogenic stop-gain (truncating) CHEK2 variants were found in 3 patients (4.5%), plus a novel missense variant in CHEK2 of uncertain significance. ATM variants of uncertain significance were found in another 4.5%. The cohort is small, but the 4.5% truncating-variant rate is notable and adds to evidence that CHEK2 contributes to early-onset breast cancer beyond the European founder populations where c.1100delC predominates — relevant to risk assessment in South Asian families, where CHEK2 data remain sparse.

**Baseline check:** Nothing today changes established risk estimates (truncating variants ~2–2.7x breast cancer OR / 20–30% lifetime risk; I157T/S428F low-penetrance OR ~1.3; ~2x prostate risk; CHEK2 is not a Li-Fraumeni gene).
