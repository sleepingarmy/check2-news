# CHEK2 Daily Report — 2026-09-29

## TL;DR
A new MGH/Harvard study in *European Radiology* found that prostate cancer patients carrying germline pathogenic variants — CHEK2 being the single most common gene in their cohort (21%) — show more aggressive MRI features and are more often upgraded to higher-grade disease at surgery. Relevant to CHEK2 carriers, whose prostate cancer risk is already ~2x baseline.

## New Research Publications

**2026-09-28 — "Clinical and MRI characteristics of germline pathogenic variant carriers diagnosed with prostate cancer"** — *European Radiology* — [DOI: 10.1007/s00330-026-12918-5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00330-026-12918-5)

A Massachusetts General Hospital retrospective case-control study compared 62 prostate cancer patients carrying germline pathogenic variants (PVs) with 246 sporadic controls. CHEK2 was the most frequently mutated gene in the carrier group (21.0%), ahead of BRCA2 (19.4%). PV carriers were significantly more likely to receive the most suspicious MRI score (PI-RADS 5; OR 2.7), to have higher-grade extra-prostatic extension (OR 3.0), and to be reclassified to a higher grade at prostatectomy (37% vs 20%). For CHEK2 carriers, this reinforces that hereditary prostate cancers — including CHEK2-associated ones — tend to behave more aggressively, supporting MRI-based early-detection strategies rather than relaxed screening. This does not change the established baseline (~2x prostate cancer risk for CHEK2 PV carriers) but adds imaging/pathology nuance about tumor behavior once cancer develops.
